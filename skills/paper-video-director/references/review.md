# Review rubric v7

Run separate evidence/plan review, rendered-frame review, and narrative/timing review. Each review states what it actually inspected. Frame sampling cannot prove all intervening motion; text and duration cannot establish pronunciation or naturalness by listening.

Evidence: check identity, primary-source quotes, numbers, baseline, scope, limitations, contradictions and unsupported claims. A polished but unsupported claim blocks release.

Rendered frames: inspect actual encoded frames at shot boundaries and representative midpoints against the corresponding narration. Check source identity, readable diagrams/tables, crop/highlight correctness, subtitles/labels obscuring content, empty frames and a misleading match between words and visuals. A guessed issue without visible evidence is a warning, not an error.

Distinguish a sourced factual report from a promise that the viewer can directly observe or compare something in the selected picture. A supported fact may accompany a relevant illustration of the same subject and conditions; that picture does not have to independently prove every narrated fact, provided it does not imply a false observation or comparison. Unsupported facts, a wrong subject or condition, a false causal claim, misleading loss of essential context, or failure to deliver a promised observation or essential comparison remain errors: identify the exact claim and visible conflict. Extra related panels, nonessential crop remnants, or details established by source text but not independently visible in the illustration are warnings when they do not cause such a conflict or prevent understanding an essential step. This does not make unrelated imagery adequate evidence or relax explicit presentation requirements.

Apply the user's actual visual settings. When `minimal_text=true`, a PDF body paragraph, web screenshot or text-heavy source image is subject to the same requirement as a generated text card. A clearly visible paragraph wall that contradicts this requirement is a content-presentation error, even if the narration's facts are accurate. A necessary short quotation being explained at a readable pace, paper identity, or essential chart axes/legends is not automatically a violation. Do not demand source prose on screen merely because it supplies the evidence quote.

Evaluate the meaningful subject inside the crop, not just how far the background or source rectangle fills the canvas. For multi-panel strips, identify which action, object or relationship the narration requires the viewer to discern. If actual frames show that it cannot be discerned at the supplied scale, report an error tied to that shot's explanatory task; suggest a single-panel crop or a short sequence of relevant crops, preserving needed comparison context. Do not classify every wide image, static shot or low area percentage as an error. Background fill, a highlight border or a larger outer rectangle is not evidence that the subject became readable. Keep required chart axes/legends and avoid fixing readability by removing evidence.

For these errors, state the block/shot and sampled time, what is actually visible, the relevant narration and measured shot duration, and the concrete conflict or loss of information. Small secondary labels or a merely preferred composition remain warnings when the key content is readable and no explicit requirement is violated. At plan review, use supplied source images and the planned crop to identify definite violations; do not invent encoded occlusion or measured timing before rendering. Flag uncertain legibility for rendered review instead of guessing a failure.

When planned canvas previews are attached, compare them with full sources and actual crops: a large source preview is not its on-screen size. Native-pixel image and highlight rectangles help locate the relevant comparison but are not measurements of semantic subject size and impose no automatic area threshold. Prefer a discernible single panel or necessary adjacent comparison while retaining shared labels and conditions. For portrait staged diagrams, inspect whether vertical or multi-row organization makes the current relation clear with stable node positions; neither a fixed vertical stack nor filling every blank area is required. Planned previews omit real captions, timing and motion, and omitted previews remain uninspected.

Judge the layers the selected layout actually shows. With `layout_style="immersive"`, shot.label is not drawn. For an immersive shot with `protect_content=true` (for example, a PDF page exported as an image), chapter is not drawn either. These are editor metadata: hidden metadata cannot restore missing axes, conditions or legends. A repair that changes only those hidden fields has not restored the missing visible context; inspect the actual crop/canvas or encoded frame before crediting the fix.

For `column_crop` page/image shots, compare the attached full original with the actual same-scale composition. Dashed gutters mark omitted horizontal regions; it is not an untouched continuous figure. Verify the selected columns retain their original order, shared row alignment, relevant headers/conditions and necessary axes/legends. Missing context that makes the central comparison misleading remains an error; the mere presence of a faithful, visibly separated composition is not an error. Check readability in the selected canvas, not just the source raster. Do not credit hidden label/chapter metadata for restoring information omitted by the composition, or demand decorative template labels.

Keep an error when the missing context still makes a central claim misleading or prevents the promised observation or essential comparison. Name the block/shot, missing condition and visible evidence; do not fail a scene merely because an optional label is absent. A sourced report still need not be independently proved by every picture. Request a source view that preserves the needed context readably, or a relevant readable overview; if the selected canvas cannot support it, report the constraint and suggest a user decision. Do not change the user-selected aspect ratio or layout automatically, require template annotations, or weaken the factual check to accept an invisible repair.

Story/timing: can a viewer understand the selected subject and follow the claims at the intended depth? For research, check whether relevant prior context, the research point, mechanism and result are sufficiently connected. Check narrative continuity, jargon, repetitive text, rushed numeric passages, long silent tails and excessive static holds using actual timeline durations. Balance readability, depth and pace; do not reward exhaustive background at the expense of the explanation. Do not call measured audio statistics an auditory review.

The interface's target seconds are an approximate planning budget, not a hard deadline. Actual duration follows recorded narration and complete actions. Unless the user explicitly requires a maximum in the creative brief, a modest deviation (for example about 100 seconds for a 90-second target) is not an error and must not by itself make ready=false. At most note a concrete pacing improvement as a warning. Do not shorten correct explanations, factual qualifiers or complete actions merely to hit the target. Genuine long silent tails, unusably rushed delivery, or an explicitly required hard maximum remain separate, evidence-based issues.

In `duration_mode=adaptive`, there is no fixed seconds target; a stored seconds value is only memory for switching back to target mode. Review the content's scope, actual speech, complete actions and pacing without inventing a time limit or requiring padding. Mode changes do not authorize rewriting protected narration or changing the selected speech rate.

For paper and research explainers, use **常见方法、具体问题、本文解决什么、方法怎样起作用、效果如何** as coverage questions. Do not enforce that order, equal emphasis, five scene titles or five separate sections. Prior-method context may be brief or merged with a mechanism comparison when sufficient for understanding. `problem_solution` is an optional structure, not a mandatory review template. Verify that a complete paper explanation introduces the paper/team/task early. A hook or example should support the selected research point. Compare the actual media inventory against claims about research coverage; a capped selection is not an exhaustive survey.

Test the central explanation with a brief source-grounded retelling from the narration and visible material, using only the coverage questions relevant to its scope. Ask whether the viewer has enough context to understand what changed, why it matters and how the evidence bears on that claim. A counterfactual can help test a causal explanation when supported by the source; neither that exercise nor an ablation is required for every story.

Treat a missing relationship as an error only when it makes a central claim misleading, unsupported or incomprehensible within the declared scope. Identify the absent relationship, relevant block (or transition between blocks), and source passage that establishes its importance. Less relevant background, an abbreviated prior-method description, or an alternative ordering is not automatically an error; record an actionable warning only when adding context would materially help. A task demonstration, component input/output list or collection of numbers cannot substitute for a central causal connection the video claims to explain. Do not use a generic "story not clear" judgment. Verify any baseline actually compared and distinguish claimed contributions from reused background components. Do not demand all five coverage items in every excerpt or story.

Inspect mechanism scenes at stage transitions as well as representative midpoints. Verify the depicted operations against the source and preserve any before/after relationship actually being compared. Do not demand a comparison diagram where the story does not require one. Distinguish explanatory illustrations from measured results and separate method phases when that distinction matters. Conditional example: in a learning-system video, a prediction must not appear as measured truth, and a training feedback path must not appear as the deployed system's execution path. Do not require learning, feedback or deployment phases in unrelated research. Wrong direction, role, condition or causal claim is an error when supported by the visible frame and source. Static imagery that merely relates to the topic, excess boxes, small secondary labels, or a late but non-misleading transition are specific improvement warnings unless they prevent understanding an essential step or create a false claim.

For the result, check that the metric actually tests the narrated benefit and preserve the baseline and comparison conditions. Separate a supported improvement from the stronger claim that a particular component caused it. Record untested explanations as such; do not turn a proposed mechanism or uncontrolled comparison into causal proof.

When the user's creative brief requests paper triage before close reading, check whether the actual explanation supports that decision for the stated interests. Necessary prior context, the concrete research point, the operative mechanism, the scope of results and relevant limitations may be brief, merged or reordered; do not require five chapters or a generic “reading value” verdict. Verify that proposed reasons to read follow from specific source-backed content, distinguish interpretation from observation, and do not rely on venue, popularity or institutional prestige. Do not reward an invented score, unsupported ranking or “must read” label. A reported task score is not a universal quality score or a fair cross-paper comparison by itself.

For this optional purpose, a misleading central reading recommendation or a mechanism/result claim unsupported by its source is an error under the same evidence rules. Missing optional reading pointers or less relevant implementation details are not automatic failures. Check that matched visuals actually explain the claimed mechanism or show its evidence rather than merely depict the broad topic. A vague teaser can warrant a specific warning about missing decision-relevant information, not an invented judgment that the paper is poor. Protected scripts and excerpts keep their existing fidelity and scope rules; a request to screen an article or media item does not turn it into a research paper.

For general article explainers, assess the article's own argument, chronology or causal structure; do not demand a paper, experiment, repository or the five-part research framework when none is appropriate. Supplied scripts remain protected unless the user requested rewriting: verify fidelity and flag source conflicts or explanatory gaps without changing the words. For excerpts identified in task metadata or delivery notes, inspect all included claims and continuity, but do not fail them merely for omitting parts of a complete research explanation. Keep that scope description out of burned-in production labels.

Save each round with the plan hash and video SHA256. If a specific error is repairable, repair within the shared limit of three revision generations per input across schema, plan, timing and final review; the initial normal generation does not count, and failed revision generations do. Review each valid candidate before accepting it and repeat both rendered reviews after a new render. If review is unavailable, retain the candidate and resume that review without another generation; repeated clicks do not reset the budget or reroll an already rejected unchanged candidate. Do not rewrite protected user narration. An unresolved error remains a review-needed draft. Do not manufacture passing reviews.

Also bind the final review to the actual render configuration and voice/rate/display settings. Changed settings or media invalidate the prior review, even when narration text is unchanged. Inspect the last encoded frame for unintended end cards and the first paper identity shot for unexplained quantitative comparisons.

Check chapter labels for editing-language scaffolding such as “先讲清” or “核心想法”; prefer the actual subject and preserve factual qualifiers. A review that did not run, timed out or could not access its inputs is pending or unavailable, never passed. Record image sampling, transcript checks, timing checks and actual listening separately.
